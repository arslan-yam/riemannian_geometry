# Labeling-based and bound-based distance query techniques as structural analogues of the Certified Cut Index (CCI)

Verification legend used throughout:
- **[V]** verified this session from the source text (fetched page or PDF, or the Bast et al. survey text that was extracted).
- **[S]** seen only as a search snippet or secondary description, not read in the primary paper.
- **[K]** stated from background knowledge of the literature. The citation/DOI is given, but the text was **not** re-read this session. The report writer should treat [K] items as "standard knowledge, check before quoting."

CCI recap for comparison: a recursive kd hierarchy of straight axis-parallel cut lines over the 2D chart; portals sampled on each cut inside a bounded window with geometrically growing spacing; each vertex stores EXACT continuous geodesic distances to portals of its ancestor cuts; near pairs stored exactly; query at the deepest separating cut gives UB = min_j l(s,p_j)+l(t,p_j) and LB = max(chord, min_j (f_j+f_{j+1})/2 - arcgap_j), using 1-Lipschitz label rows along the cut; (1-eps)d <= LB <= d <= UB <= (1+eps)d; capped variant refines exactly when the interval is too wide.

## Q1. Graph labeling and partition methods (H2H, TEDI, CH-based HL, hub labels, PLL, Gavoille separator labels, CRP, TNR): what they store and how they bound queries

### Takeaway
Every exact road-network labeling scheme (HL, CH-HL, PLL, H2H, TEDI, PHL) stores exact distances to a *finite discrete* set of hubs or separator vertices, and its **cover property** makes the min-over-common-hubs query exactly equal to d. No lower bound is needed, and none is computed. Partition methods (CRP, TNR) store distances to boundary or transit vertices and are also exact. TNR is the closest relative of CCI's "near pairs exact / far pairs via cut" split: it has a locality filter with an exact fallback. None of them samples a continuous cut, so none faces CCI's problem of bounding d from below between samples.

### Cited Findings
- **Hub Labeling (HL).** Each vertex u stores hubs with exact distances. The cover property requires that L(s)∩L(t) contain a vertex on a shortest s–t path, so dist(s,t) = min over common hubs u of dist(s,u)+dist(u,t). The query is a merge-like linear sweep, about five memory accesses on road networks. [V] — [Bast et al., "Route Planning in Transportation Networks", arXiv 1504.05140 (2015/2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140)
- **CH-based hub labels.** Abraham et al. define the label of u as the upward search space of a CH query from u, with suboptimal entries removed. Any vertex order defines a labeling. [V] — [Bast et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140). Primary sources [K]: Abraham, Delling, Goldberg, Werneck, "A Hub-Based Labeling Algorithm for Shortest Paths on Road Networks", SEA 2011 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-20662-7_20)); "Hierarchical Hub Labelings for Shortest Paths", ESA 2012 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-33090-2_4)).
- **Pruned Landmark Labeling (PLL).** It runs a BFS/Dijkstra from each vertex in a fixed order and prunes when the existing labels already certify the distance, giving an exact 2-hop cover. [K] — [Akiba, Iwata, Yoshida, "Fast exact shortest-path distance queries on large networks by pruned landmark labeling", SIGMOD 2013](https://doi.org/10.1145/2463676.2465315). The survey confirms that pruned labeling is used in PHL preprocessing. [V] — [Bast et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140)
- **Pruned Highway Labeling (PHL).** It decomposes the graph into disjoint shortest paths and labels each vertex with distances to vertices on a few of those paths. Any shortest s–t path is s–u–w–t with u–w a subpath of a shared path. The survey notes "some similarity with Thorup's distance oracle for planar graphs" without needing planarity. PHL is exact and discrete. [V] — [Bast et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140). PHL is the graph method closest to "labels to points on a separator line", but the path vertices are all stored, not sampled.
- **H2H (Ouyang, Qin, Chang, Lin, Zhang, Zhu, SIGMOD 2018, pp. 709–724).** It builds a tree decomposition. Each internal tree node corresponds to a vertex cut of G. Each vertex label stores a distance array (exact distances to all its tree-decomposition ancestors) and a position array. A query uses the LCA of s and t in the tree and takes the min over the LCA's bag vertices (a vertex cut separating s and t). The result is exact. [S] for the mechanism (abstract and secondary sources); [K] for the LCA/position-array detail — [UTS repository record](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/127287); [DOI 10.1145/3183713.3196913](https://doi.org/10.1145/3183713.3196913). Extended version: VLDB Journal 32:1263–1287 (2023) — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00778-023-00789-x) [S].
- **Hierarchical Cut Labelling (HC2L)** (Farhan, Koehler, Ohsaka, Wang, SIGMOD 2023 / arXiv 2311.11063) is a later balanced-cut-tree labeling that cites H2H as a tree-decomposition labelling method. Vertices store distances to the vertices of their ancestor cuts, and a query takes the min over the cut at the LCA. It is exact and discrete. [S] — [arXiv 2311.11063](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.11063). *Inference:* HC2L is structurally **even closer to CCI than H2H**: a balanced-cut hierarchy, labels to ancestor-cut vertices, and a query at the LCA cut. The paper should cite it alongside H2H.
- **TEDI** (Wei, SIGMOD 2010) is an exact tree-decomposition-based index. Vertices store distances within bags, and a query runs over the tree path. [K] — [doi 10.1145/1807167.1807181](https://doi.org/10.1145/1807167.1807181)
- **Customizable Route Planning / multilevel overlay.** It builds a balanced arc-separator partition into cells. Each cell holds a full clique of shortcut arcs between its boundary vertices, and the query runs Dijkstra on the source and target cells plus the overlay. It is exact. HiTi was the earlier version, tested on grid graphs. [V] — [Bast et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140). Primary [K]: Delling, Goldberg, Pajor, Werneck, "Customizable Route Planning in Road Networks", Transportation Science 51(2), 2017 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2014.0579)); SEA 2011 version.
- **Transit Node Routing (TNR).** It precomputes a distance table among transit nodes T plus each vertex's access nodes A(u) with distances. A global query takes min over a(s), a(t) of d(s,a(s)) + D[a(s),a(t)] + d(a(t),t). A **locality filter** detects queries that may not pass through T and falls back to an exact search (typically CH). Grid-based separators give natural locality filters. [V] — [Bast et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140); primary [K]: Bast, Funke, Sanders, Matijevic, Schultes, "In Transit to Constant Time Shortest-Path Queries in Road Networks", ALENEX 2007; Bast, Funke, Sanders, Schultes, Science 316(5824), 2007 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1137521)).
- **Precomputed Cluster Distances (PCD).** It stores cell-to-cell distances, and the query is Dijkstra pruned with the lower bound dist(s,u) + dist(C(u),C(t)) + dist(v,t) against the best current upper bound. [V] — [Bast et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140). *Inference:* PCD is the one partition method that explicitly keeps an UB/LB pair. The LB is used for pruning, though, not returned as a certified interval.
- **Gavoille, Peleg, Pérennes, Raz, "Distance labeling in graphs"** (J. Algorithms 53, 2004). Separator-based exact labels: a vertex stores distances to all vertices of the separators of its ancestor regions, which gives O(sqrt(n) log n)-bit-scale labels for planar graphs (O(log² n) words for trees and bounded treewidth). [K] — [doi 10.1016/j.jalgor.2004.05.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgor.2004.05.002). This is the theoretical ancestor of H2H and HC2L, and of CCI's "distances to ancestor-cut portals". It stores all separator vertices.

### Inferences
- CCI's exact upper bound UB = min_j l(s,p_j)+l(t,p_j) is the hub-labeling / H2H query formula restricted to the deepest separating cut. That justifies the H2H analogy. The difference is that CCI's "hubs" are a sampled finite subset of a continuum, so the cover property fails and the min is only an upper bound. The (1+eps) guarantee then has to come from a geometric portal-spacing argument, as in Thorup/Klein (Q3), not from a cover argument.
- No exact labeling scheme returns a lower bound, because none needs one. CCI's two-sided interval is therefore not inherited from the graph-labeling tradition.
- CCI's "near pairs stored exactly / capped variant refines exactly" corresponds to TNR's locality filter plus exact fallback. That is worth acknowledging.

### Gaps
- H2H and TEDI full texts were not opened this session, so the mechanism details are [S]/[K].
- I did not verify whether HC2L (2023) or later H2H follow-ups (e.g., dynamic H2H variants, 2021–2025) include any approximate/sampled variant. None was seen in the snippets.

## Q2. ALT / landmark lower bounds, and their use for geodesics and terrains

### Takeaway
ALT gives a valid lower bound max_l |d(u,l) − d(t,l)| from exact landmark distances via the triangle inequality. It is a global-landmark, non-separator bound. Landmark sketches (e.g., Potamias et al.) give upper bounds via d(s,l)+d(l,t). The sketch pair is a two-sided interval in principle, but it has no (1±eps) guarantee. I found no source applying ALT to exact continuous geodesics on triangle meshes. CCI's LB is a different mechanism: it relies on the s–t path having to *cross* the cut, plus Lipschitz interpolation along the cut, not on a triangle inequality through a landmark.

### Cited Findings
- ALT picks a landmark set L and stores the distances from every vertex to and from each landmark. It uses dist(u,t) ≥ dist(u,l) − dist(t,l) and dist(u,t) ≥ dist(l,t) − dist(l,u), takes the max over landmarks as a feasible A* potential, and is exact as a search. [V] — [Bast et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140); primary [K]: Goldberg & Harrelson, "Computing the shortest path: A* search meets graph theory", SODA 2005 ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/1070432.1070455)).
- Geographic (Euclidean / max-speed) lower bounds are valid but weak on road networks. [V] — [Bast et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140). *Relevant analogue:* CCI's "chord bound" is the continuous analogue of this geographic bound.
- Extensions: ALP / polygon-inequality landmarks tighten the bounds on graphs. [S] — [arXiv 1603.01607](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.01607). Landmark-based A* has been used on robot terrain costmaps (grids, not exact geodesics). [S] — [Murphy & Newman, ICRA 2011](https://ori.ox.ac.uk/media/5774/2011icra_murphy.pdf).
- Landmark-based distance *estimation* (upper bound min_l d(s,l)+d(l,t), plus a triangle-inequality lower bound) appears in large-network estimation, e.g., Potamias, Bonchi, Castillo, Gionis, "Fast shortest path distance estimation in large networks", CIKM 2009. [K] — [doi 10.1145/1645953.1646063](https://doi.org/10.1145/1645953.1646063). It gives no approximation guarantee.
- Graphics analogue: Xin, Ying, He, "Constant-time all-pairs geodesic distance query on triangle meshes", I3D 2012. It uses a geodesic Delaunay triangulation of samples, stores pairwise sample distances plus each vertex's distances to the 3 corners of its geodesic triangle, and answers queries by planar unfolding. The answer is a heuristic estimate, with no certified bound. [S] — [dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/si3d/XinYH12.html); [secondary description, Cardiff TVCG](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/143769/3/NEWGeodesicEmbedding-TVCG.pdf).

### Inferences
- CCI's LB uses the cut as a *mandatory crossing set* (any s–t path crosses the separating line), which is a "min over the separator" lower bound, plus a continuity argument between samples. ALT is a "max over landmarks" difference bound. The two are logically different. CCI could optionally combine its LB with ALT-style bounds from its stored labels, since the labels give d(s,p) and d(t,p) for the same p, so |l(s,p) − l(t,p)| ≤ d holds for free. Note, however, that ALT-type bounds are usually weak for points on opposite sides of a separator.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for "landmark lower bounds for exact geodesic distance on terrain/meshes". This appears absent or obscure (a negative finding, with moderate confidence after 2 searches).

## Q3. Thorup/Klein eps-covers and portals for planar distance oracles; is there Lipschitz interpolation between portals for a lower bound?

### Takeaway
Thorup's (and Klein's) (1+eps) planar oracles are the closest **theoretical** analogue of CCI's portal mechanism. They use recursive shortest-path separators, and each vertex stores exact distances to O(1/eps) "portals" (connections) per separator path at each ancestor level. A query takes the min over portal pairs plus the distance along the path. That min is a pure upper bound, and the guarantee comes from portal-spacing tied to d(v, path). In every source examined, the lower bound is just the true distance. None interpolates between consecutive portals with a Lipschitz argument to get a returned, certified LB. The 2026 polygonal-domain extension (van der Laan, Staals, Theunissen) explicitly has no interpolation and no LB either.

### Cited Findings
- Thorup (FOCS 2001 / JACM 2004) and Klein (SODA 2002) independently gave (1+eps)-approximate planar distance oracles with O(n log n / eps) space and O(1/eps) query time. [S] — [Le, NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10507312); [Weimann–Yuster](https://www.cs.haifa.ac.il/~oren/Publications/planar-diam.pdf). Primary [K]: Thorup, "Compact oracles for reachability and approximate distances in planar digraphs", JACM 51(6), 2004 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/1039488.1039493)).
- The key idea is to use portals along each shortest path of a shortest-path separator. For a vertex v and a path Q, O(1/eps) portals on Q suffice so that every vertex on Q is reached within (1+eps) via some portal. [S] — [arXiv 1601.00839](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00839); [arXiv 2111.03560 (Le, FOCS 2021)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03560)
- A practical portal construction places portals along the path at spacing equal to a fixed fraction of the path's length. [S] — [arXiv 1308.3336](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.3336). *Inference:* Thorup's per-vertex construction picks portals greedily with spacing relative to d(v, portal), so the portal density is higher near v's closest point on Q. That is a discrete cousin of CCI's "window with geometrically growing spacing". The window plus geometric growth is the classic way to obtain an O(log(1/eps)/eps) or O(1/eps) portal count.
- Separators have at most 3 shortest paths, and the recursion is a balanced binary tree of depth O(log n). [S] — [arXiv 1104.5214](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.5214)
- Kawarabayashi–Sommer–Thorup: with additive stretch eps·D, portals become global per separator rather than per vertex. [S] — [arXiv 2207.05659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05659)
- Le (FOCS 2021): any (1+eps) oracle for weighted planar graphs needs Ω(n) space. [S] — [FOCS 2021 PDF](https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2021-Papers/pdfs/FOCS2021-5stbVHiOp5jRHWlSl41FkR/205500a373/205500a373.pdf)
- Targeted search for "Lipschitz interpolation between portals / certified interval" in the distance-oracle literature returned nothing matching. [V: negative search result] — search over arXiv/LIPIcs hits, including [arXiv 2207.05659](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.05659) and [ESA 2017](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol087-esa2017/LIPIcs.ESA.2017.25/LIPIcs.ESA.2017.25.pdf)

### Inferences
- The mathematical fact CCI uses (that v ↦ d(s, q(x)) is 1-Lipschitz in arc length x along the cut, so between consecutive portals d(s,q) ≥ max(f_j − |x−x_j|, f_{j+1} − |x−x_{j+1}|), whose min over the gap is (f_j+f_{j+1})/2 − gap/2) is elementary. It is implicitly what makes Thorup-style portal *upper* bounds work. However, I found no oracle that **returns** this as a lower bound. *Note for the authors:* make sure "arcgap_j" in the LB formula equals half the arc gap, or the gap with matching convention, and that the Lipschitz constant is 1 for *geodesic* distance restricted to the cut. That holds because d(s,·) is 1-Lipschitz w.r.t. the geodesic metric, and the geodesic distance between two cut points is ≤ the arc length along the cut, as long as the cut segment lies in the domain. In graphs the analogous statement only holds along a path's own edges, which may explain why the graph literature never uses it.
- The structural novelty claim most at risk is "labels to sampled portals on ancestor separators + min-over-portals UB with (1+eps) guarantee": Thorup/Klein (graphs) and the 2026 polygonal-domain paper (continuous) already do this. The novel part appears to be the **certified LB from Lipschitz interpolation, the resulting per-query two-sided interval, and its use to trigger exact refinement**, together with straight axis-parallel cuts (not shortest-path separators) on a terrain chart.

## Q4. Continuous-space analogues: obstacles in the plane and polyhedral/terrain surfaces with separators and sampled boundary points

### Takeaway
There is a long line of separator + Steiner/boundary-point oracles in continuous domains: Arikati et al. 1996, Chen 1995, Aleksandrov et al. 2010, Djidjev–Sommer 2011, EAR-Oracle 2023, van der Laan–Staals–Theunissen 2026, and Gaur–Inkulu 2026. All of them return an **upper bound / approximate value only**. The closest is **van der Laan, Staals, Theunissen (arXiv, Mar 2026)**. It is a Thorup-style recursive separator in a polygonal domain with O(1/eps) anchor points per vertex per separator, storing path lengths to anchors, and a min-over-anchor-pairs query giving d ≤ d̂ ≤ (1+eps)d. It has no LB and no interpolation, and its separators are geodesic shortest paths, not straight cuts. I found no prior work using straight axis-parallel geometric cuts with window-based geometric portal spacing **and** a certified Lipschitz lower bound.

### Cited Findings
- **van der Laan, Staals, Theunissen, "Approximate Dynamic Nearest Neighbor Searching in a Polygonal Domain", arXiv 2603.11775 (12 Mar 2026).** It recursively splits the polygonal domain with Thorup-style separators of ≤3 shortest paths (tree height O(log n)). Each polygon vertex gets O(k), k = ⌈1/eps⌉, anchor points on separator Q, each with weight equal to the length of a real obstacle-avoiding path to it. Distances along Q are precomputed from an endpoint. Query: min over anchors a of s and b of t of d̂_a + d̂_b + d(a,b). The result is d ≤ d̂ ≤ (1+eps)d, a two-point distance oracle with O(n/eps · log n) space and O(1/eps² · log n) query. The paper does not present separate lower bounds or certified intervals and uses no Lipschitz interpolation. [V] — [arXiv 2603.11775](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11775v1)
- **Gaur & Inkulu, "Two-point Approximate Shortest Path Queries among Convex Polygonal Obstacles in the Plane", arXiv 2608.09677 (10 Aug 2026).** The output path has (1+eps) multiplicative and 13ℓ additive stretch, with O(n + h/√eps·(h+1/eps)) space. [V: abstract and intro only] — [arXiv 2608.09677](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09677). Its intro surveys the two-point-query lineage. [V]
  - Chen (1995): (6+eps)-approximate two-point queries, O(n log n + n/eps) space. [V via Gaur–Inkulu intro]
  - Arikati et al. (ESA 1996): (√2+eps) approximation with a space/query trade-off (O(n²) space or O(√n) query), plus (2√2+eps) and (3√2+eps) variants. [V via Gaur–Inkulu intro]
  - Exact two-point queries: Chiang & Mitchell (O(n⁵) space or O(n+h⁵)) and Guo et al. (O(n²) space, O(h log n) query). [V via Gaur–Inkulu intro]
- **Arikati, Chen, Chew, Das, Smid, Zaroliagis, "Planar spanners and approximate shortest path queries among obstacles in the plane", ESA 1996, LNCS 1136, pp. 514–528.** It builds an L1 2-spanner among obstacles without Steiner points, and uses planar-graph separator-based query structures on the spanner. [S] — [preprint PDF](https://ranger.uta.edu/~gdas/websitepages/preprints-papers/arikati.pdf); [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/ArikatiCCDSZ96)
- **Aleksandrov, Djidjev, Guo, Maheshwari, Nussbaum, Sack, "Algorithms for approximate shortest path queries on weighted polyhedral surfaces", DCG 2010.** It places Steiner points with geometric spacing near vertices, applies a graph separator to the resulting Steiner graph, and gives (1+eps) approximate queries on polyhedral 2-manifolds. [S] — [MaRDI record](https://portal.mardi4nfdi.de/wiki/Publication:603866)
- **Djidjev & Sommer, ESA 2011 ("Approximate distance queries for weighted polyhedral surfaces").** It places Steiner points on triangle bisectors, spaced geometrically near mesh vertices, and builds a graph whose edge weights are local distances. It is a (1+eps) oracle built on Thorup-style planar separator techniques. [S] — [ESA 2011 PDF](https://public.lanl.gov/djidjev/papers/esa11-quries.pdf) (fetch failed this session)
- **EAR-Oracle (Huang, Wei, Wong, Tang, SIGMOD 2023).** It partitions the terrain into boxes and indexes distances from boundary "highway" nodes to Steiner points for arbitrary point-to-point queries. It is a (1+eps) approximation with no certified LB. [S] — [paper PDF](https://home.cse.hkust.edu.hk/~raywong/paper/sigmod23-EAR-Oracle-terrain.pdf); [ACM doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3588694). SE-Oracle (Wei et al., SIGMOD 2017) and SP-Oracle (Djidjev–Sommer 2011) are cited there as index-based predecessors. [S]
- Lanthier, Maheshwari, Sack (Algorithmica 2001) is the origin of Steiner-point-on-edges approximation for weighted polyhedral surfaces: it builds a graph and runs Dijkstra, with no query index. [K] — [doi 10.1007/s00453-001-0027-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-001-0027-5)
- Hershberger & Suri (SICOMP 1999) give continuous Dijkstra via a quadtree-style conforming subdivision for single-source Euclidean shortest paths in O(n log n). It builds a shortest path map, not a two-point label oracle. [V as cited in the Gaur–Inkulu intro] — [arXiv 2608.09677](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09677)

### Inferences
- CCI's straight axis-parallel cuts are a real difference from this line of work. Thorup-style geometric oracles must use **shortest-path separators**, because their "distance along the separator" term d(a,b) is exact only if Q is a geodesic. CCI avoids needing d(a,b) along the cut by computing UB through a single portal (min_j l(s,p_j)+l(t,p_j), a two-hop / H2H-style formula). It then gets a lower bound from the 1-Lipschitz argument, which only needs geodesic distance between cut points to be ≤ the arc length along the cut. That holds for any straight segment inside the domain. This is a coherent reason why straight cuts plus Lipschitz LB go together, and it is plausibly novel.
- Geometric (window-based, growing) portal spacing has precedents. Aleksandrov et al. and Djidjev–Sommer place Steiner points with geometric progressions near vertices, and Thorup's portal choice is per-vertex and distance-relative. CCI's window-based geometric spacing on cuts is in the same family, so it should not be claimed as novel by itself.

### Gaps
- Did not read Chen 1995 or Arikati et al. 1996 in full to confirm whether they use straight-line cuts with sampled points (the Arikati et al. √2-type factors suggest L1/spanner arguments, not straight-cut portals). Moderate confidence that neither gives an LB.
- The Djidjev–Sommer ESA 2011 PDF fetch failed, so details are [S].
- Sun & Reif (BUSHWHACK, 2006) were not checked this session. [K]: they give single-source (1+eps) weighted-region paths via Steiner points, with no oracle and no LB.

## Q5. Any work (any field) giving per-query certified lower+upper intervals for shortest-path or geodesic distance

### Takeaway
I found no prior oracle that returns a per-query certified two-sided interval [LB, UB] with (1−eps)d ≤ LB and UB ≤ (1+eps)d. That holds for graph labeling, planar/geometric oracles, terrain oracles, graphics geodesic-query methods, or eikonal/fast-marching domain decomposition. Two-sided bounds do appear *internally*: PCD (LB for pruning vs best UB), TNR (locality filter plus exact fallback), ALT (LB only) and landmark sketches (UB + triangle LB, with no guarantee). These appear as search devices, not as a returned certificate with a guaranteed relative width.

### Cited Findings
- PCD uses a valid lower bound per visited vertex, compares it to the best current upper bound, and prunes. [V] — [Bast et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140)
- TNR stays correct even when the locality filter misclassifies, because of its exact fallback. [V] — [Bast et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140)
- The van der Laan et al. 2026 continuous oracle explicitly provides no separate LB or certified interval. [V] — [arXiv 2603.11775](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11775v1)
- Fast-marching domain decomposition (Yang & Stern, JCP 332, 2017; Diamantopoulos et al.; Breuss et al. 2011) partitions grids into subdomains with ghost/interface layers for *parallel computation* of a single distance field. It stores no interface-point distance tables for query answering and gives no interval. [S] — [arXiv 1502.07303](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.07303); [Diamantopoulos et al.](https://www.iue.tuwien.ac.at/pdf/ib_2019/JB2019_Diamantopoulos_2.pdf)
- The Xin–Ying–He 2012 graphics oracle returns an unfolded estimate without bounds. [S] — [secondary description](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/143769/3/NEWGeodesicEmbedding-TVCG.pdf)

### Inferences
- Novelty assessment of the specific combination. Individually, prior work covers:
  - (a) hierarchical separators with labels to ancestor separators: Gavoille et al. 2004, H2H 2018, HC2L 2023;
  - (b) sampled portals with (1+eps) guarantee: Thorup/Klein 2001–04, Aleksandrov et al. 2010, Djidjev–Sommer 2011, van der Laan et al. 2026;
  - (c) geometric Steiner spacing: Aleksandrov et al., Lanthier et al.;
  - (d) exact fallback for local queries: TNR.

  I found **no** work combining straight-line geometric cuts of a continuous domain, window-based geometrically spaced portals, exact continuous labels, and a **returned per-query certified LB from 1-Lipschitz interpolation between consecutive portals**, nor one that uses the resulting interval width to trigger exact refinement. The safest novelty claim is the certified two-sided interval and its use, plus straight cuts as an enabling design choice. "Portals on separators" and "H2H-like labels" should be framed as inherited.
- Positioning suggestion. "Analogue of H2H on a continuous surface" is fair for the *query shape* (LCA cut, min over cut). Reviewers who know Thorup will say "this is Thorup/Klein portals on a surface". The paper should cite Thorup 2004, Klein 2002, van der Laan–Staals–Theunissen 2026, Aleksandrov et al. 2010, Djidjev–Sommer 2011, EAR-Oracle 2023 and HC2L 2023, and position the LB/certificate as the differentiator.
