# Certification, not separators, carries the novelty claim

The Certified Cut Index (CCI) is novel as a combination, but most of its parts are inherited, and the paper has to be positioned around certification rather than around its separator hierarchy. Several ingredients already exist: the recursive separator hierarchy, labels from each vertex to sampled portals on its ancestor separators, the min-over-cut query, exact near pairs, and even axis-parallel kd cuts. Gotsman & Hormann's SIGGRAPH Asia 2022 "SEP" paper has all of them, the last only as an explicit suggestion in its conclusion. Thorup's planar and polygonal-domain oracles, Djidjev & Sommer, H2H and HC2L supply the theoretical and database ancestry. Per-query lower and upper bounds against the exact terrain geodesic also exist, in Kaul et al. (PVLDB 2013/2016), but they are computed on the fly with a constant ratio. Across four parallel searches, the searchers found no work that combines four things: straight chart cuts justified by a window/offset-cost lemma instead of shortest-path separators; exact continuous MMP/VTP labels; a returned per-query lower bound from 1-Lipschitz interpolation between portals, giving an interval that is (1±ε)-tight against the exact surface geodesic; and use of that interval to drive certify-or-refine and to decide kNN and range queries exactly. Reviewers will raise one objection immediately: any oracle whose estimate is a realizable path length already implies the interval [est/(1+ε), est]. So the claim cannot be "the first certified interval". It must be an adaptive, index-backed interval whose measured width is far below the worst case, valid against the exact continuous metric, and usable for exact decisions at microsecond cost. Two framings should be dropped: "first separator-label oracle for geodesics" and "first terrain LB/UB".

## Four components are new; the scaffolding around them is inherited

The searchers found prior art for every structural element taken individually. The **separator hierarchy with distances to ancestor separators** goes back to Gavoille et al. 2004 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalgor.2004.05.002), unverified) and runs through H2H, HC2L and SEP. **Sampled portals with a (1+ε) guarantee** come from Thorup/Klein, Aleksandrov et al., Djidjev–Sommer and van der Laan et al. **Geometrically spaced sample points** come from Aleksandrov–Maheshwari–Sack Steiner placement and Thorup's distance-relative portal choice. **Exact handling of local queries with a fallback** echoes Transit Node Routing's locality filter ([Bast et al. survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05140)). **Filter-and-refine with LB/UB for surface kNN** dates to Deng et al. ICDE 2006 ([PDF](https://infolab.usc.edu/csci599/Fall2009/papers/Surface%20k-NN%20Query%20Processing.pdf)), Shahabi et al. PVLDB 2008 ([PDF](https://infolab.usc.edu/csci599/Fall2009/papers/Indexing%20land%20surface%20for%20efficient%20kNN%20query.pdf)) and Kaul. The UB formula min_j l(s,p_j) + l(t,p_j) is the hub-labelling query restricted to one cut.

Four things appear new. They fall into two groups.

**A design and proof technique.** Every separator–portal oracle the searchers found uses shortest-path or graph separators. The reason is that its query adds an along-separator distance d(a,b) that is exact only when the separator is a geodesic ([van der Laan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11775v1)).
- **(1) Straight chart cuts with the window lemma.** The CCI cuts with straight lines that are not geodesics. The window lemma (crossings confined within μ/λ of the box; offset c costs ≥ 2λc) replaces Thorup's ε-cover argument and is what licenses the spacing max(g, 2ελc). No precedent with a crossing-cost-derived window was found. Arora-style portals on straight dissection lines are the nearest idea, but they come from Euclidean TSP approximation, not geodesic oracles (recalled, unverified).
- **(2) Exact continuous labels from local sweeps.** SEP fits polynomials, the Steiner oracles store discretized distances, and EAR stores A-SSAD approximations.

**A query-side contribution.**
- **(3) A returned per-query lower bound from 1-Lipschitz label rows.** A targeted search for Lipschitz interpolation between portals in the oracle literature returned nothing, and van der Laan et al. explicitly provide no separate LB.
- **(4) Use of the interval to certify-or-refine and to decide kNN and range queries exactly.** RC-Oracle and SE answer "(1+ε)-approximate kNN" ([TODS 2026](https://yanyinzhao.github.io/files/TODS2025-PointCloudOracles-paper.pdf)).

There is a coherent technical reason why (1) and (3) go together, and the paper should make it explicit. A single-portal two-hop UB needs no along-cut distance, so the cut need not be a geodesic. The Lipschitz LB needs only that the geodesic distance between two cut points be at most the arc length along the cut, which holds for any straight segment lying in the domain. The window lemma is new as a device, but it rests on a terrain distortion constant λ. It therefore trades the generality of shortest-path separators for terrain-specific geometry, and the paper should say so plainly.

One further inference strengthens the SEP contrast; it is ours and has not been checked against either paper's proofs. SEP must scan *all* ancestor curves because its separator distances are restricted to the owning patch, and the true path can leave the patch (its Fig. 3) ([Gotsman & Hormann 2022](https://www.inf.usi.ch/hormann/papers/Gotsman.2022.CGI.pdf)). A CCI cut is a full straight line across the chart, and its labels are unrestricted surface distances. Any s–t path between opposite sides must therefore cross that line, and the window lemma bounds where. If the CCI query really uses only the deepest separating cut, this is a concrete, provable simplification over SEP worth stating.

## Reviewers will raise eight objections, and each has a defensible answer

The most dangerous objection is the first one, because it is correct as stated. The answers below are calibrated so that none overclaims.

| Expected objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Any path-length (1+ε) oracle already implies the interval [est/(1+ε), est]." | Concede it explicitly. Then argue three points. (a) The implied interval is valid against the exact geodesic only when the oracle's guarantee is. EAR's holds up to an additive δ and against approximate A-SSAD distances ([EAR](https://home.cse.hkust.edu.hk/~raywong/paper/sigmod23-EAR-Oracle-terrain.pdf)), and RC/TI's holds against a point-cloud graph, so their implied intervals are not certificates for the surface geodesic. Djidjev–Sommer's, by contrast, would be valid. (b) The CCI's LB is computed from data per query and is typically far tighter than est/(1+ε); show the distribution of measured UB/LB against the a priori ε. (c) The adaptive width is what lets the capped index refine only the rare wide queries and decide kNN/range answers exactly. |
| "Kaul et al. already give LB and UB on terrain distance." | Agree that the filter-and-refine paradigm and the exact-geodesic LB are theirs, and cite both papers. The difference is that Kaul computes a face-crossing path per pair on the fly with a fixed ratio λ = 1 − ε/(K+c) and no index ([Kaul 2016](https://home.cse.hkust.edu.hk/~raywong/paper/vldb16-NewLowerUpperBound-Terrain.pdf)). The CCI's interval comes from a precomputed index in ~1 µs with (1−ε)d ≤ LB. Frame certify-or-refine as "Kaul's filter-and-refine with index-backed (1±ε) intervals". Kaul's per-pair bound time was not extracted; measure it as a baseline rather than asserting a ratio. |
| "SEP already does separator labels on meshes, and even suggests kd cuts." | Agree, and cite SEP prominently. SEP has no lower bound and no certificate. Its error lemma is conditional on an unenforced sup-norm fit. Its labels are lossy fits with planar interpolation, and it ran on ≤5k vertices with exact VTP ([Gotsman & Hormann 2022](https://www.inf.usi.ch/hormann/papers/Gotsman.2022.CGI.pdf)). The CCI's contributions are the window lemma that makes kd cuts provably sufficient with sparse portals, exact labels, the certified LB, and scale. Ideally, back this with SEP as a measured baseline. |
| "EAR also partitions the plane and stores boundary-node distance maps." | EAR uses a single-level uniform grid. All vertices of boundary-crossing faces become highway nodes, its distances are approximate, it queries by Dijkstra, it has an additive error term, it reports mean error only, and it has no LB ([EAR](https://home.cse.hkust.edu.hk/~raywong/paper/sigmod23-EAR-Oracle-terrain.pdf)). Say this in the paper so that a reviewer does not discover the resemblance first. |
| "This is just Thorup/Klein portals on a surface." | The label layout is Thorup's, and the paper should say so. Thorup's ε-cover lemma requires the separator to be a shortest path. The CCI's straight cuts are not, so it needs a different proof (window/offset cost) and a different query (single-portal UB plus Lipschitz LB). |
| "Djidjev–Sommer already give a (1+ε) surface oracle with near-linear space; yours is Θ(n^1.5)." | Concede the asymptotics. The CCI's claims are exact labels, a certified adaptive interval, exactness via refine, and engineering scale. Djidjev–Sommer reports no implementation the searchers found. Do not claim asymptotic superiority. |
| "The window lemma depends on λ; what if the terrain has cliffs or near-vertical faces?" | State λ as an explicit input-dependent constant, report its measured value per dataset, and explain what happens when λ is small (wider windows, more portals, and more refinement under the cap). This is the honest price of straight cuts. |
| "Your LB rests on 1-Lipschitz rows; does that hold with weights and obstacles?" | This is our inference and needs checking in the proofs. With weights, the row is Lipschitz in *weighted* arc length, with the constant set by the maximum weight along the cut. With obstacles, the arc-length argument holds only on cut sub-segments lying in free space. The Lipschitz-gap term must use the matching convention (half-gap versus full gap). A further proof obligation: exact sweeps on ellipse sub-meshes yield exact distances only if the ellipse contains every relevant shortest path. |

## Seven changes would make the claim robust

The **citations to add** fall into four groups.
- **Theory:** Thorup 2004 and ESA 2007, Klein 2002, Djidjev–Sommer 2011, Aleksandrov et al. 2010, van der Laan–Staals–Theunissen 2026, and Aleksandrov–Maheshwari–Sack 2005 (geometric Steiner spacing).
- **Graphics:** SEP 2022 and Burghard & Klein 2015.
- **Databases:** Kaul 2013 and 2016, Deng et al. 2006/2008, Shahabi et al. 2008, SE 2017 and TODS 2022, EAR 2023, UP 2024/25, RC/TI 2024/26, H2H 2018 and HC2L 2023.
- **Context:** ALT, Transit Node Routing, Gavoille et al., and possibly Arora-style portals as the straight-line-portal precedent. The searchers found none of these discussed together in prior work, which is itself a positioning opportunity.

**Add SEP as a baseline** if feasible. The graphics researcher judged it simple to reimplement over VTP. A run on the paper's terrains would turn the strongest objection into a measured win on scale, maximum error and certification. If time is short, at least compare against its published numbers on small meshes and state why a full run was not done. **Report max error and interval width everywhere**, because EAR and SEP report only mean error, and the CCI's advantage is sharpest in the tail. **Reconcile the EAR footprint:** the measured 333 GB at 0.5M faces exceeds the 256 GB machine in the EAR paper, so document the parameters (m, ζ, ε) used, or the comparison will be challenged. **Drop or soften five claims:** "first certified interval", "first separator/portal geodesic oracle", "novel geometric portal spacing" (it is a cousin of Steiner spacing), any asymptotic win over Djidjev–Sommer, and "analogue of H2H" as a *novelty* claim rather than a *design* lineage. **State λ, the ellipse-containment condition and the weighted/obstacle Lipschitz constants as explicit lemmas.** **Frame certified kNN and range queries** as exact answers with a certificate, set against the "(1+ε)-approximate kNN" of SE and RC.

## The search leaves six gaps that could still hide a threat

The **Djidjev–Sommer and Aleksandrov et al. internals** (separator type, per-vertex stored data) were not read, because the hosting servers refused connections. Their headline bounds are verified; their constructions are recalled only. **Thorup 2004, Klein 2002, H2H, HC2L and TEDI** were not read in full, so their mechanism descriptions above rest on snippets and background knowledge. **No DBLP-wide sweep of SIGMOD, PVLDB and ICDE 2025–2026** was performed. Every recent terrain paper found came from the HKUST group, so a paper from another group cannot be excluded. **EuroCG and CCCG abstracts**, robotics admissible-heuristic work and interval-arithmetic eikonal solvers were barely searched. That leaves a residual risk that someone has used a Lipschitz lower bound along sampled boundaries. **Two numbers were not extracted:** EAR's absolute index sizes at 0.5M and 1M faces (figures only), and Kaul 2016's per-pair bound computation time. **Unconfirmed details:** whether SEP has public code, and the SoCG 2026 venue of van der Laan et al.

## Conclusion

The CCI's real contribution sits in what it does at query time, not in how it partitions the terrain. Read side by side, the literature shows two separate traditions that never met. Separator–portal oracles (Thorup through SEP) deliver one-sided estimates. Bound-based methods (Kaul, Burghard & Klein, ALT) deliver intervals without index-backed (1±ε) tightness. The CCI joins them through one observation: straight cuts make an along-cut Lipschitz lower bound available, and the window lemma makes straight cuts sufficient. A paper built around that observation, with SEP and Kaul as acknowledged neighbours and measured baselines, presents a smaller target than one claiming the hierarchy itself.

The practical implication for SIGMOD/VLDB is that the certificate should be shown to do work, not just exist. Measured interval widths should sit far below ε. Exactly decided kNN and range answers should be compared against the "(1+ε)-approximate" answers of SE and RC. The refine rate under the cap should be reported. Those three figures are what make the inherited scaffolding beside the point.
